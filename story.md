# The Glitching Tales: Story & Lore

> Canon reference: see `Game_Design_Document.md` for full world, character, and chapter design.

## World Information
* **World:** Terosandia
* **Chapter 1 Region:** The Emerald Coast — home of Octo Village
* **Chapter 1 Boss:** Croton, King of Crabs (a guardian who tests the player, not a true villain)
* **Chapter 2 Region:** The Ruined Ocean — the sunken kingdom of the Underriver
* **Chapter 2 Boss:** The ghost of King Oblongtalo
* **Protagonist:** A customizable human (name, appearance, and skill type — Magick, Melee, or Range — chosen by the player)
* **Key companions:** Octa (a young octopus from Octo Village) and Elder Barnico

## Chapter 1: The Emerald Coast

The traveler woke up on the shore of the Emerald Coast with sand in their hands and no memory of how they'd gotten there — no ship, no wreckage, nothing behind them but the tide erasing their own footprints. Ahead, past the dunes, a village of round little houses waited.

Octo Village was quiet, the kind of quiet that came from everyone already knowing something was wrong. The villagers — soft, round little octopus and squid folk — watched the newcomer with a mix of curiosity and relief, as if they'd been waiting for a stranger to show up for a while now.

At the center of the village sat Elder Barnico: an octopus who looked far too young to carry a title like "Elder," and far too old in the eyes to be anyone but. He studied the traveler for a long moment, then smirked like he'd already made up his mind about something.

"So. You're the one the tides dragged in," Barnico said. "Good. We could use someone like you."

Before the traveler could ask what he meant, a small, eager octopus shoved past the gathered villagers. "Me! Me! I'm coming too!" Octa announced, as if it had already been decided.

Barnico chuckled. "Octa's right, for once. You'll need the help." His smirk faded into something more serious. "There's a crab out past the temple ruins who calls himself a king. Croton. He's been testing everyone fool enough to challenge him — and so far, everyone's failed."

"And now it's your turn," Octa grinned, already halfway out the door.

---

The road to Croton's temple wound through cracked stone archways older than the village itself, crab-shaped carvings worn smooth by centuries of tide. Croton was waiting at the end of it — a crab easily three times the traveler's height, a dented crown wedged between his eyestalks, a staff topped with a swirling, crackling orb of magick held loose in one massive claw.

"Another one," Croton rumbled, sounding almost bored. "Let's see if you're worth my time."

He was not gentle about it. Waves rose from nowhere and slammed the shore like fists, and his claws came down again and again, fast enough to keep the traveler stumbling backward. But bit by bit, hit by hit, the traveler held their ground — and Octa's shouted encouragement from the sidelines didn't hurt either.

When Croton finally lowered his staff, he was almost smiling. "Worthy," he said simply. "Huh. It's been a while." He pointed a claw out toward the horizon, where the ocean turned a shade of grey-green that didn't belong to any healthy sea. "Whatever's waiting for you next is out there. Ruined Ocean's not kind to visitors. Try not to drown."

## Chapter 2: The Ruined Ocean

Long before the village, before Croton, before any of it — there was the Underriver.

It was a kingdom built entirely beneath the waves, woven together out of coral and current and every kind of creature the ocean had ever grown, and it was ruled by a king named Oblongtalo. By every account, he was a good king. He was also, as it turned out, the only one who saw the meteor coming.

Oblongtalo gave the order the moment he sensed it — every soul in the Underriver, evacuate, now, no time to argue. And they listened. By the time the sky split open and the ocean floor buckled under the impact, the kingdom was empty.

Everyone escaped. Everyone except the king who'd warned them.

---

The traveler and Octa set out across the Ruined Ocean, and for a while it was almost peaceful — until a wave rose ahead of them that didn't move like a wave at all. It curled, closed, and came down like a fist, and the last thing the traveler saw before the water swallowed them whole was something disturbingly like fingers.

They sank a long way before they hit bottom.

The ocean floor down there was littered with the bones of the Underriver — broken coral towers, empty streets, silence where there should have been the hum of a living kingdom. And standing in the middle of the ruin was a ghost: crowned, translucent, ancient.

"My name is Oblongtalo," the ghost said, voice echoing like it was speaking from underwater and out of it at the same time. "If you wish to reach your destination, you will have to go through me. Let's see if you're strong enough to survive what my kingdom could not."

The battle that followed felt less like a fight and more like an argument the ocean itself was having — every strike from Oblongtalo carried the weight of a king who'd spent an age alone at the bottom of the sea, refusing to let anyone pass until they proved they deserved to.

When it was over, Oblongtalo's ghost went still, and something like peace crossed his translucent face.

"You are worthy," he said.

The water around the traveler began to glow, pulling them upward and outward all at once — and when the light faded, the ocean was gone. In its place: endless dunes, and the beginning of the Dunes of Force.
